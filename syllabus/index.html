English III · Junior year · Shawnee High School

Syllabus & expectations

Mr. Casey

This course is about propaganda and how the English language has changed over time: how the stories we tell about power decide which words survive. Most days open with a knights-and-knaves puzzle or a thought experiment to sharpen critical thinking, and media criticism runs through the whole year. Four books, a rhetoric unit, and a personal essay unit carry the rest.

What we'll study

  • RhetoricThe basic structure under every argument.
  • BeowulfWhat a death is worth, and who decides.
  • 1984Not the cameras. The dictionary.
  • Home FireWhose grief the world takes seriously.
  • MacbethA stolen crown, and a debt that won't close.
  • Personal narrativeYour own writing, held to the same standard.

Expectations

  • Respect: for the room, for each other, for the material.
  • Curiosity: ask the question even if it feels obvious.
  • Honesty: including about what you didn't do or don't understand.
  • Show up: on time, ready to work.

The practical stuff

  • Grading: percentage-based. For the first three marking periods, the lowest possible grade on anything is 50%, even if the real score was lower. Grades reflect your schoolwork only, not your behavior in class.
  • Homework: kept minimal on purpose, mostly grammar practice and reading. Reading checks confirm the reading got done; they're not hard tests.
  • Attendance: miss more than nine unexcused days in this year-long class, and you don't get credit for it. District policy, the same in every class.
  • Devices: phones go in the classroom's phone caddy during class time. New this year, districtwide: phones stay away from the first bell to the last, all day.
  • Academic honesty: cheating and plagiarism both start with a zero the first time it happens. That includes using AI tools without permission.
